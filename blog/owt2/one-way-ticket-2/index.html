One Way Ticket 2

Oct 14, 2025 | 3 minutes read
Share this: Twitter Facebook Pinterest

Place: Bilbao Day 2 was… intense. I have to admit that the lack of sleep has taken its toll on me, and was as super exhausted! But it did not stop me from exploring what Bilbao had to offer:)

History Bilbao is the largest city in the Basque country. From the Middle Ages, Bilbao was a key center for the export of wool and iron ore to other parts of Europe, and the city quickly became a major commercial hub. By the 16th century, Bilbao was one of the most important ports in Spain, and the city’s shipbuilding industry thrived. During the Industrial Revolution of the 19th century, Bilbao became a major center for iron and steel production.The city’s iron mines, which were located in the nearby mountains, provided a steady supply of raw materials, and Bilbao soon became one of the most important industrial centers in Europe. While the decline of heavy industry in the late 20th century led to economic difficulties in Bilbao, the city has managed to reinvent itself in recent years, becoming a hub for technology and innovation.

My opinion I found out that the city is quite walkable - which is a great plus! You can easily go from one end to another. Since the city is located in the valley, and it’s surrounded by mountains, you can count on the fact that you will walk uphill. Funny fact is, that in Bilbao they even have a street called “San Francisco street” - and if you’ve been to SF, you will know exactly why:D (yes, it’s because it’s very steep). Another fact I loved is that the city is filled with cute, local tavernas, and you can sit in any of them and just enjoy a glass of wine, coffee or get some pintxos, the famous local version of finger food. From the typically recommended sights, I definily can recommend a visit to the Erriberako Merkatua! I visited at the morning (11 ish, right when the market opens), and it was full of local people (or at least I think so) getting a cut of fresh fish or fresh vegetable. Then, the Guggenheim Museum was also great to see! I loved that the walk to the museum was very cute, it was near a river and all, and if I had more time in the city, I’d definitely go for a run around the museum! The museum itself is spectacular, it’s huge, and I love that they incorporate modern elements into it - i.e. creating a virtual que for one part of the exhibicion (You just scan a qr code, it directs you to an app, that tells you how many people are before you, so you can roam freely and enjoy other exhibicions, and when it’s your time in line, the app sends you a push notifications). Another plan I did not have time to do is to watch the Goggenheim building at sunset, because I believe it has to be an amazing view!
